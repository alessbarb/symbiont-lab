# Mathematical map

The mathematical compendium should be read as formal support for mechanisms described elsewhere, not as a parallel version of the organism.

The current ten-part compendium covers four broad areas.

**Perception and non-stationarity.** Online moments, correlation, EWMA-like dispersion estimates, regime shifts and acclimation provide the mathematical basis for learning what is ordinary without retaining every raw observation.

**Attention and bounded allocation.** Budgeted selection mechanisms formalise the fact that sensing and cognition are finite resources. Greedy allocation is an implementation choice with different guarantees from exact knapsack optimisation and should be described as such.

**Belief, evidence and social trust.** Bayesian-style pseudo-observation updates, conflict accumulation, leave-one-out agreement and source reliability formalise evidence revision while preserving disagreement and source identity.

**Cognition and plasticity.** Recurrent graph activation, gating, normalisation, structural pressure, causal selection and statistical evaluation explain how internal organisation can change while remaining bounded.

A reader should normally encounter these equations after the corresponding scientific concept. The compendium remains the place for derivations, numerical caveats and proofs.
