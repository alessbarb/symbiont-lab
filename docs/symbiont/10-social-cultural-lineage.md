# Social, cultural and lineage processes

Symbiont has three distinct ways in which information can cross individual boundaries: social evidence during life, cultural transmission, and biological-style heredity. They must not be collapsed into one mechanism.

## Social evidence

The social subsystem represents sources, relations, evidence and exchange. Claims retain source identity so that agreement among dependent reports is not automatically treated as independent confirmation. Trust-like quantities are therefore evidence properties, not permanent moral labels attached to another organism.

The runtime can receive signed messages through a consent-bound channel and a replay guard. Incoming cultural claims incur a cognition cost before being admitted to the epistemic ledger. The system therefore treats communication as a resource-consuming event with provenance, not as free global memory.

## Cultural agency

Outbound communication is currently bounded by social availability and metabolic pressure. The runtime may broadcast claims when peers exist and pressure is sufficiently low. This is an implemented policy constraint; it should not be overinterpreted as a complete theory of social motivation.

## Culture versus heredity

A learned fact transmitted after birth is cultural even if it later persists for a long time. Germline inheritance is different: it passes bounded hereditary material through genome and lineage mechanisms. The distinction allows experiments to ask whether a behaviour recurs because descendants inherited a predisposition, because they were taught, or because they independently relearned it.

## Genome and germline

Genome v2 separates identity, loci, inheritance state and epigenetic marks. The germline is intentionally narrower than the organism's acquired cognitive state. Reproduction therefore creates a new individual with inherited capacities and priors rather than cloning the parent's lived cognition.

## Lineage

Lineage records parent/descendant relationships and supports evolutionary experiments without making the laboratory the author of individual cognition. Mutation and recombination may alter hereditary material, while postnatal development remains responsible for acquired structure.
