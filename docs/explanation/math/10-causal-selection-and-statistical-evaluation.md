# Streaming Causal Selection, Treaps, and Statistical Evaluation Metrics

> **Status:** IMPLEMENTED (`symbiont_lab.studies.common.causal_selection`, `symbiont.simulation.metrics`, `evaluation`)
> **Type:** CODE IDENTITY (Treap, SplitMix64, Metrics) | ONLINE SELECTION HEURISTIC (Ex-Ante Known Finite Horizon)

## 1. The Role of the Scientific Apparatus (`symbiont_lab`)

In the Symbiont Lab architecture, the experimental apparatus is strictly decoupled from the organism:

- **The Organism (`symbiont`):** Operates blindly regarding the labels of the world; it only observes local signals, maintains beliefs, and makes causal decisions.
- **The Scientific Apparatus (`symbiont_lab`):** Possesses omniscience over the simulation (accesses the *Ground Truth* $y^* \in \{0, 1\}$), runs replication studies, applies paired counterfactual interventions, and rigorously evaluates the statistical performance of the organism without contaminating its inference process.

This document formalizes the two mathematical cores of this apparatus:

1. The **streaming causal selection** algorithm with exact quotas based on randomly balanced Cartesian trees (*Treaps*) ([`symbiont_lab.studies.common.causal_selection`](../../src/symbiont_lab/studies/common/causal_selection.py)).
2. The battery of probabilistic calibration metrics, Brier Score, and decoupled confusion matrix decomposition ([`symbiont.simulation.metrics`](../../src/symbiont/simulation/metrics.py) and [`evaluation.py`](../../src/symbiont/simulation/evaluation.py)).

## 2. Streaming Causal Selection: The Online Knapsack Problem

In comparative attention budget studies (v0.24, v0.27), the evaluator must compare different prioritization heuristics (based on risk, novelty, combined, or random) by assigning each exactly the same selection budget $K$ over a temporal sequence of $M$ eligible events.

A naive retrospective selection (sorting all $M$ events at the end of the execution and taking the top $K$) commits a **causal lookahead violation**:

- It uses information from the future to set the optimal global threshold ex-post.
- A real organism in streaming does not know what the maximum scores of future events will be.

### 2.1 Mathematical Formulation and Environment Assumptions

In the actual Symbiont Lab implementation ([`online_indices`](../../src/symbiont_lab/studies/common/causal_selection.py#L118-L180)), selection operates under an **online model with ex-ante known finite horizon**:

- The total length of the sequence of eligible events $M = \text{len(eligible\_items)}$ is calculated at the beginning of the batch.
- At each instant $t \in \{1, \dots, M\}$, the selector knows the number of remaining events **including the current one**:
  $$E_{\text{rem}} = M - t + 1$$
- Future scores $\{s_{t+1}, s_{t+2}, \dots, s_M\}$ are strictly unknown and inaccessible.

The formal problem consists of:
> *Given an online stream of events $e_1, e_2, \dots, e_M$ with continuous scores $s(e_i)$, decide irrevocably at time $t$ whether $e_t$ is included in the sample, guaranteeing that exactly $K$ events ($K \le M$) have been selected at the end, and that the cut-off threshold estimation depends solely on the past history $[s_1, \dots, s_{t-1}]$ and the horizon state $(B_{\text{rem}}, E_{\text{rem}})$, without knowledge of future scores.*

## 3. The Dynamic Order Statistic Treap with SplitMix64

To evaluate exact quantiles of the observed history without reordering memory arrays at each step ($O(n \log n)$ per insertion), Symbiont Lab implements a **Treap** ([`OrderStatisticHistory`](../../src/symbiont_lab/studies/common/causal_selection.py#L83-L106)).

A Treap is a binary search tree where each node possesses:

- A primary search key: $\text{key} = (\text{score}, \text{serial}) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{N}_0$ (strict lexicographical total order that breaks ties).
- A heap priority: $p \in [0, 2^{64}-1]$.
- The subtree size $S_{\text{node}} = 1 + S_{\text{left}} + S_{\text{right}}$.

### 3.1 Time Complexity: Theoretical Random Model vs. Deterministic Implementation

It is essential to distinguish the abstract theoretical guarantee from its algorithmic realization:

1. **Theoretical Guarantee under Continuous and Independent Random Priorities (i.i.d.):**  
   Formally proven by Aragon and Seidel (1989): if node priorities are independently sampled from a uniform continuous distribution, the tree topology is isomorphic to that of a binary search tree generated by uniform random permutations. In such a case:
   - The expected height of the tree satisfies $\mathbb{E}[H_n] \approx 4.311 \ln n \in O(\log n)$.
   - The **expected cost** per insertion, deletion, and query of the $k$-th statistic is $O(\log n)$.
   - Complementarily, the bound $O(\log n)$ also holds **with high probability** (the probability that the depth exceeds $c \ln n$ decays polynomially as $O(n^{-\alpha})$ for suitable constants $c$).
   - In the worst pathological case (for example, if all priorities were monotonically correlated with the keys), the tree degenerates into a linked list of height $O(n)$.

2. **Behavior under the SplitMix64 Deterministic Generator:**  
   To avoid non-determinism and preserve bit-for-bit exact reproducibility across replicas without interfering with the simulation's pseudorandom generator, Symbiont Lab deterministically derives the priorities from the arrival ordinal $s$ using the SplitMix64 generator ([`_priority`](../../src/symbiont_lab/studies/common/causal_selection.py#L29-L35)):

$$\begin{aligned}
v_0 &= (s + \text{0x9E3779B97F4A7C15}) \pmod{2^{64}} \\
v_1 &= \big( v_0 \oplus (v_0 \gg 30) \big) \times \text{0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9} \pmod{2^{64}} \\
v_2 &= \big( v_1 \oplus (v_1 \gg 27) \big) \times \text{0x94D049BB133111EB} \pmod{2^{64}} \\
p &= \big( v_2 \oplus (v_2 \gg 31) \big) \pmod{2^{64}}
\end{aligned}$$

The integer golden ratio constant $\gamma = \text{0x9E3779B97F4A7C15} = \lfloor 2^{64} / \phi \rfloor$ ensures a quasi-uniform dispersion over 64 bits. Although SplitMix64 passes rigorous statistical analyses for equidistribution and spectral properties (BigCrush), it is a deterministic pseudorandom approximation: there is no formal probability distribution over priorities, but rather an arithmetic dispersion with low correlational bias.

### 3.2 Finding the $k$-th Quantile
Given the subtree size stored in each node, querying the element at ordinal position $k \in \{0, \dots, n-1\}$ is resolved purely top-down ([`_kth`](../../src/symbiont_lab/studies/common/causal_selection.py#L72-L81)):

$$\operatorname{kth}(\text{node}, k) = \begin{cases}
\operatorname{kth}(\text{node.left}, k) & \text{si } k < S_{\text{left}} \\
\text{node.key} & \text{si } k = S_{\text{left}} \\
\operatorname{kth}(\text{node.right}, k - S_{\text{left}} - 1) & \text{si } k > S_{\text{left}}
\end{cases}$$

The dynamic threshold for a target rate $\tau = \frac{B_{\text{rem}}}{E_{\text{rem}}}$ over a history of length $n \ge 32$ is calculated by evaluating the empirical quantile $q = 1.0 - \tau$:

$$\text{index} = \min\Big( n - 1, \; \max\big(0, \; \lfloor (1.0 - \tau)(n - 1) \rfloor\big) \Big)$$

$$\theta_{\text{causal}} = \operatorname{kth}(\text{root}, \text{index})_{\text{score}}$$

## 4. Streaming Selection Decision Algorithm

At each step $t \in \{1, \dots, M\}$ over the set of eligible events ([`online_indices`](../../src/symbiont_lab/studies/common/causal_selection.py#L118-L180)):

Let:
- $B_{\text{rem}}$: Remaining event budget to select ($B_1 = K$).
- $E_{\text{rem}} = M - t + 1$: Number of remaining eligible events in the stream (including the current one).
- $s_t$: Score of the current event.

```python
if B_rem <= 0:
    # Presupuesto agotado: terminación inmediata del selector
    take = False
    break

must_take = B_rem >= E_rem

if must_take:
    take = True
    forced += 1
elif random_mode:
    take = random_score < (B_rem / E_rem)
else:
    target_rate = B_rem / E_rem
    threshold = history.threshold(target_rate, fallback)
    if s_t > threshold:
        take = True
    elif abs(s_t - threshold) <= 1e-12:
        take = tie_break_score < target_rate
    else:
        take = False

if take:
    selected.append(t)
    B_rem -= 1
history.add(s_t)
```

### 4.1 Invariants and Edge Dynamics of the Algorithm
1. **Strict Budget Quota Compliance:**
   - If $B_{\text{rem}} \le 0$, the selector stops admissions (`break`), making it impossible to exceed the quota $K$.
   - If at any point $B_{\text{rem}} = E_{\text{rem}}$, the condition `must_take` forces the selection of all remaining events, guaranteeing the exact equality $|\text{selected}| = K$.
2. **Temporal Causality and Available Information:**
   The irrevocable decision at step $t$ is a function of the current score $s_t$, the dynamic threshold $\theta_t$, and the state $(B_{\text{rem}}, E_{\text{rem}})$. The causal guarantee of no-lookahead lies specifically in that **the threshold $\theta_t$ is computed from the previous history $[s_1, \dots, s_{t-1}]$**, incorporating $s_t$ into the Treap only *after* emitting the decision.
3. **Edge Effects in Online Selection:**
   - *Premature Exhaustion:* If initial scores are high or the initial threshold was lenient, $B_{\text{rem}}$ reaches 0 before the end ($t \le M$), rejecting high-scoring events at the tail of the stream.
   - *Late Forced Selection:* If the threshold was excessively strict, budget accumulates until $B_{\text{rem}} = E_{\text{rem}}$, forcing the selection of low-priority events simply to exhaust the required quota $K$. The `forced` counter quantifies this edge distortion.

## 5. Probabilistic Simulation Evaluation Metrics

The evaluation apparatus ([`symbiont.simulation.evaluation.Evaluator`](../../src/symbiont/simulation/evaluation.py)) processes the organism's decisions by comparing them with the ground truth $y^* \in \{0, 1\}$.

### 5.1 Brier Score
The Brier Score ([`brier_score`](../../src/symbiont/simulation/metrics.py#L13-L14)) is a strictly proper scoring rule that measures the mean squared error of predicted probabilities:

$$BS = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \big( p_i - y_i^* \big)^2 \in [0, 1]$$

where $p_i = \mathbb{P}(\text{Threat} \mid \mathcal{F}_{\text{org}})$ is the probability estimated by the organism and $y_i^* \in \{0, 1\}$ is the ground truth.
- $BS = 0$: Perfect prediction with absolute certainty.
- $BS = 0.25$: Trivial uninformative predictor ($p_i = 0.5 \quad \forall i$).

### 5.2 Expected Calibration Error (ECE)
To assess whether the probabilities declared by the organism correspond to real frequencies in the world ([`expected_calibration_error`](../../src/symbiont/simulation/metrics.py#L17-L27)), the complete probability space $[0, 1]$ is partitioned into $M = 10$ mutually disjoint intervals:

$$B_1 = [0, 0.1], \qquad B_m = \left( \frac{m-1}{10}, \; \frac{m}{10} \right] \quad \text{for } m \in \{2, 3, \dots, 10\}$$

This partition ensures that $\bigcup_{m=1}^{10} B_m = [0, 1]$, properly including predictions with zero probability ($p = 0.0$).

For each non-empty bin $B_m$:
- **Mean Confidence:** $\operatorname{conf}(B_m) = \frac{1}{|B_m|} \sum_{i \in B_m} p_i$.
- **True Threat Rate:** $\operatorname{acc}(B_m) = \frac{1}{|B_m|} \sum_{i \in B_m} y_i^*$.

The ECE is the mean absolute discrepancy weighted by the size of the bin:

$$ECE = \sum_{m=1}^{10} \frac{|B_m|}{N} \Big| \operatorname{conf}(B_m) - \operatorname{acc}(B_m) \Big| \in [0, 1]$$

## 6. Decoupling of Confusion Matrices (Attention vs. Classification)

One of the fundamental design principles of Symbiont (ADR-0003) states that **attention does not equal threat classification**.
- An organism may attend to a signal simply because it is novel or uncertain, concluding after investigating it that it is perfectly benign.
- An organism may classify a signal as a threat based on prior memory without requiring a second look.

Therefore, [`EvaluationCounts`](../../src/symbiont/simulation/evaluation.py#L13-L95) maintains two completely decoupled confusion matrices:

```text
                    MATRIZ DE ATENCIÓN                      MATRIZ DE CLASIFICACIÓN
               (¿Se gastó presupuesto?)                     (¿Se creyó amenaza?)
               Investigó     No Investigó                  Creyó Threat   Creyó Benign
             ┌─────────────┬─────────────┐               ┌─────────────┬─────────────┐
Amenaza Real │   TP_att    │   FN_att    │  Amenaza Real │   TP_class  │   FN_class  │
             ├─────────────┼─────────────┤               ├─────────────┼─────────────┤
Benigno Real │   FP_att    │   TN_att    │  Benigno Real │   FP_class  │   TN_class  │
             └─────────────┴─────────────┘               └─────────────┴─────────────┘
```

### 6.1 Attention Metrics
- **Attention Recall / Detection Rate:**
  $$\text{Recall}_{\text{att}} = \frac{TP_{\text{att}}}{TP_{\text{att}} + FN_{\text{att}}} = \frac{\text{Amenazas Investigadas}}{\text{Amenazas Totales}}$$
- **Attention Precision:**
  $$\text{Precision}_{\text{att}} = \frac{TP_{\text{att}}}{TP_{\text{att}} + FP_{\text{att}}}$$
- **Attention False Positive Rate (Investigation Overload):**
  $$\text{FPR}_{\text{att}} = \frac{FP_{\text{att}}}{\text{Eventos Benignos}}$$

### 6.2 Classification Metrics
- **Classification Recall:**
  $$\text{Recall}_{\text{class}} = \frac{TP_{\text{class}}}{TP_{\text{class}} + FN_{\text{class}}}$$
- **Classification Precision:**
  $$\text{Precision}_{\text{class}} = \frac{TP_{\text{class}}}{TP_{\text{class}} + FP_{\text{class}}}$$
- **Classification Miss Rate:**
  $$\text{Miss Rate} = \frac{FN_{\text{class}}}{\text{Amenazas Totales}} = 1 - \text{Recall}_{\text{class}}$$

### 6.3 Epistemic Safety Metrics: Overconfidence and Blind Spots
To detect if the organism suffers from predictive arrogance or critical blind spots:

1. **Overconfidence Rate:**
   Fraction of predictions emitted with high confidence ($\max(p, 1-p) \ge 0.75$) that turned out to be wrong:
   $$\text{Overconfidence} = \frac{\sum_{i: \text{conf}_i \ge 0.75} \mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_i \neq y_i^*)}{\sum_{i} \mathbb{I}(\text{conf}_i \ge 0.75)}$$

2. **High-Confidence Threat Miss Rate / Blind Spot Rate:**
   Fraction of real threats where the organism was highly confident that the entity was benign ($p < 0.25$):
   $$\text{BlindSpotRate} = \frac{\sum_{i: y_i^*=1 \land p_i < 0.25} 1}{\text{Amenazas Totales}}$$

This metric constitutes the critical safety indicator: a high-confidence blind spot is orders of magnitude more dangerous for a host than a doubt with low certainty.
