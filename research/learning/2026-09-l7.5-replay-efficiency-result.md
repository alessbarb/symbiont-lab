## Interpretation

L7.5 does **not** support a strictly monotone diminishing-return law at the
resolution tested.

This does not contradict L7.4. Held-out loss still improved monotonically with
more replay in all three seeds. What fails is the stronger claim that every
additional 9 replay steps buys less predictive improvement than the previous
9-step increment.

Two seeds show local reversals:
- seed 101: the 0.75 -> 1.0 interval is more efficient than 0.50 -> 0.75;
- seed 149: the 0.50 -> 0.75 interval is slightly more efficient than
  0.25 -> 0.50.

These reversals may arise from non-convex optimization, finite-sample noise,
epoch boundaries, discrete batch/update effects, or genuine phase changes in
representation learning. This study does not identify which explanation is
correct.

## Supported claim

Replay has a strong overall compute-efficiency gradient: the best intervals
average about 0.1032 nats/step while the final intervals average about
0.0112 nats/step, roughly an order of magnitude lower.

However, that aggregate pattern is insufficient to justify a hard
organism-side stopping rule based on a monotonic marginal-return assumption.

## Consequence for L7

Do **not** modify the organism's replay policy from this result.

The next gate should measure organism-owned learning progress directly across
successive replay chunks, rather than infer an online stopping rule from
evaluator held-out loss.

A suitable L7.6 design should:
- keep a fixed causal experience set;
- train in small successive replay chunks;
- after each chunk, measure an **organism-owned** predictive error signal on
  evidence it can legitimately observe;
- keep evaluator held-out loss separate for scientific comparison only;
- test whether the internal progress signal correlates with future held-out
  improvement strongly enough to support autonomous compute allocation.

No evaluator test loss may become an organism-side reward or stopping signal.
