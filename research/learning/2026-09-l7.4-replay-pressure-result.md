## Interpretation

The result supports a genuine replay-compute dose response under this protocol:
for every preregistered seed, held-out predictive loss falls monotonically as
the replay budget rises while causal experience is held fixed.

The effect is not linear. Most of the gain occurs at lower/intermediate
pressures, and marginal returns shrink substantially near the top of the range.

Accuracy is not monotonic and was preregistered as secondary. In particular,
accuracy peaks at pressure 0.50 or 0.75 in these seeds even while log loss
continues to improve. This is compatible with better-calibrated probability
mass without more top-1 hits, and should not be reinterpreted as a failure of
the primary predictive-loss gate.

## Supported claim

More substrate compute can let the organism extract more predictive information
from the same lived experience, without advancing the world or injecting new
evidence.

L7.3 established the endpoint contrast. L7.4 establishes an interior
dose-response curve.

## Remaining limitation

The current L7.2 mapping is still heuristic. A pressure of 1.0 always purchases
the maximum budget even when the last increments have very small marginal value.

The next question is therefore not whether more replay helps. It is whether the
organism can allocate compute efficiently.

## Next gate — L7.5 marginal compute efficiency

Preregister a matched study that measures loss reduction per additional replay
step across the pressure intervals.

The organism-side policy should eventually be allowed to stop requesting
additional replay when its internally observable learning progress per compute
falls below a constitutional opportunity-cost threshold.

The threshold must be based on organism-owned prediction improvement and
metabolic/compute cost, never on evaluator test loss.
